✎Problems

  1. (§5.1) Decide which sentences are statements, and explain briefly: (a) "Rizal Technological University is in Mandaluyong." (b) "Did you submit the activity?" (c) "\(2x - 1 = 9\)." (d) "Every even number greater than 2 is the sum of two primes." Then, with \(p\): "I study tonight", \(q\): "I pass the long exam", \(r\): "I go out with friends", write in symbols: (e) "If I study tonight and I do not go out with friends, then I pass the long exam." (f) "I pass the long exam if and only if I study tonight."

  2. (§5.1) Write the negation of each statement in words. (a) All engineering students own a scientific calculator. (b) Some typhoons that enter the Philippine Area of Responsibility are weak. (c) No jeepney in our route is air-conditioned. (d) There is a student who answered every item correctly.

  3. (§5.2) Construct the truth table of \(\neg(p \wedge q) \vee r\). In how many rows is it true? Is it a tautology, a self-contradiction, or neither?

  4. (§5.2) (a) Use a truth table to verify De Morgan’s law \(\neg(p \vee q) \equiv \neg p \wedge \neg q\). (b) Show that \((p \rightarrow q) \vee (q \rightarrow p)\) is a tautology. Explain in a sentence why this makes sense.

  5. (§5.3) Let \(p\) be true, \(q\) false and \(r\) true. Find the truth value of (a) \((p \wedge q) \rightarrow r\), (b) \((p \leftrightarrow \neg q) \wedge (r \rightarrow q)\), (c) \(\neg(p \rightarrow r) \vee q\).

  6. (§5.3) Write the negation of each, without using the words "it is not the case that": (a) If PAGASA raises Signal No. 2, then classes are suspended. (b) I take the jeepney or I take the bus. (c) The store is open and the printer works.

  7. (§5.4) For "If a triangle is equilateral, then it is isosceles", write the converse, the inverse and the contrapositive, and give the truth value of all four statements. Then rewrite "You can join the varsity team only if you maintain a GWA of 2.00 or better" in if-then form, and state its contrapositive.

  8. (§5.5) Use a truth table to decide whether the argument is valid: \(p \vee q,\;\; \neg q \rightarrow r,\;\; \neg r,\;\; \therefore p\).

  9. (§5.5) Write each argument in symbols, name its form, and state whether it is valid. (a) If the dam’s water level exceeds the spilling level, then the gates are opened. The gates were not opened. So the level did not exceed the spilling level. (b) If I commute by MRT, I arrive early. I did not commute by MRT. So I did not arrive early. (c) If I finish the problem set, I can review for the exam. If I can review for the exam, I will pass. Therefore if I finish the problem set, I will pass. (d) The meeting is on Tuesday or on Thursday. It is not on Tuesday. So it is on Thursday.

  10. (§5.6) Use Euler diagrams to determine whether each argument is valid; draw the diagrams. (a) All mammals breathe air. No fish is a mammal. Therefore no fish breathes air. (b) No honest person is a cheater. Some students are honest. Therefore some students are not cheaters.

  11. (§6.1) A graph has vertices \(A, B, C, D, E, F\) and edges \(AB, AF, BC, BE, BF, CD, CE, DE, EF\). (a) Draw it and list the degree of each vertex. (b) Verify that the degree sum is twice the number of edges. (c) Does it have an Euler circuit or an Euler path? Find one if it exists.

  12. (§6.1) (a) How many edges does the complete graph \(K_8\) have, and what is the degree of each vertex? (b) For which values of \(n\) does \(K_n\) have an Euler circuit? Explain. (c) Explain the difference between an Euler circuit and a Hamiltonian circuit with an example from city life.

  13. (§6.2) Five towns \(A, B, C, D, E\) are joined by roads with these distances (km): \(AB = 8\), \(AC = 12\), \(AD = 6\), \(AE = 10\), \(BC = 5\), \(BD = 9\), \(BE = 7\), \(CD = 11\), \(CE = 4\), \(DE = 13\). (a) How many distinct Hamiltonian circuits are there? (b) Find the circuit given by the nearest-neighbor algorithm starting at \(A\), and its length. (c) Find the circuit given by the edge-picking algorithm, and its length. (d) Which is shorter? Is either one optimal?

  14. (§6.3) (a) A connected planar graph has 10 vertices, each of degree 3. How many edges and how many faces does it have? (b) Use the inequality \(e \le 3v - 6\) to prove that \(K_6\) is not planar. (c) Explain why this inequality cannot be used to prove that \(K_{3,3}\) is non-planar.

  15. (§6.4) Seven student organizations \(M, P, C, E, H, B, A\) must each hold a general assembly, and two organizations that share members cannot meet at the same time. The pairs that share members are \(MP, MC, ME, PC, PH, CH, CB, EB, HB, EA, AB\). (a) Draw the conflict graph. (b) Find its chromatic number and a schedule using the fewest time slots. Explain why fewer slots are impossible.
