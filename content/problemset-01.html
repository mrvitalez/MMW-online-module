✎Problems

  1. (§1.1) Find the next two terms of each pattern and state the rule in words: (a) 2, 6, 18, 54, … (b) 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, … (c) 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, … (d) 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, …

  2. (§1.2) (a) List the Fibonacci numbers \(F_1\) to \(F_{20}\). (b) Verify that \(F_1 + F_2 + \cdots + F_{10} = F_{12} - 1\). (c) Verify that \(F_1^2 + F_2^2 + \cdots + F_8^2 = F_8 F_9\), and explain the identity with a drawing of Fibonacci squares.

  3. (§1.2) Use Binet's formula \(F_n = \dfrac{\varphi^n - \psi^n}{\sqrt5}\) to compute \(F_{25}\), showing \(\varphi^{25}\) and \(\psi^{25}\) to at least 4 significant figures. Check your answer by continuing the list from Problem 2.

  4. (§1.2) (a) Show algebraically that \(\varphi = \frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\) satisfies \(\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1\) and \(\frac{1}{\varphi} = \varphi - 1\). (b) Give \(\varphi\) to 6 decimal places. (c) A golden rectangle is 34 cm wide. How long should it be, to the nearest cm? Why is the answer a Fibonacci number?

  5. (§1.3) Triangles in a row are made from matchsticks: 1 triangle uses 3 sticks, 2 triangles use 5, 3 triangles use 7. (a) Write a formula for the number of sticks \(s(n)\) needed for \(n\) triangles. (b) How many sticks are needed for 50 triangles? (c) How many triangles can be made with exactly 251 sticks? (d) Is the pattern arithmetic or geometric? Justify.

  6. (§1.4) A ball is thrown straight up from a height of 1.2 m at 9.8 m/s, so \(h(t) = 1.2 + 9.8t - 4.9t^2\) (meters, seconds). (a) Find its height after 0.5 s. (b) When does it reach its maximum height, and what is that height? (c) When does it hit the ground? (d) State one assumption of the model.

  7. (§1.4) A town has 25,000 residents and grows by 2.5% per year. (a) Predict its population after 10 years. (b) After how many years will the population double? (c) A second census after 10 years counts 30,500 people. What does this say about the model?

  8. (§1.5) Ana deposits ₱20,000 for 5 years. Bank X pays 3% simple interest per year; Bank Y pays 3% interest compounded annually. (a) Find the amount in each bank after 5 years. (b) Which is better and by how much? (c) Explain how this is an example of mathematics used “for human ends”.

  9. (§1.5–1.6, explain) Choose one real problem in your community (for example flooding, traffic, waste, electricity bills). In one paragraph: (1) state the problem, (2) explain how mathematics is used to address it, (3) name the mathematical concepts involved, and (4) give a concrete example. Then explain why it is more accurate to say people use mathematics to predict, optimize and influence the situation than to say mathematics controls it.

  10. (§2.1–2.2) Classify each as an expression, an open sentence or a closed sentence, and give the truth value of each closed sentence: (a) \(4x - 7\) (b) \(4(3) - 7 = 5\) (c) \(x^2 + 1 > 0\) (d) \(2 + 2 \times 2 = 8\) (e) the product of 6 and \(n\) (f) \(\{1, 2\} \subseteq \{1, 2, 3\}\).

  11. (§2.3) Evaluate using the order of operations and show each step: (a) \(48 \div 4 \times 3 - 2^3\) (b) \(-3^2 + (6 - 2 \times 5)^2 \div 4\) (c) \(\dfrac{5 + 3 \times 4}{2^2 - 3} + 10 \div 5 \times 2\).

  12. (§2.4) Let \(U = \{1, 2, \dots, 15\}\), \(A = \{x \in U \mid x \text{ is odd}\}\) and \(B = \{x \in U \mid x \text{ is a multiple of } 3\}\). (a) Write \(A\) and \(B\) in roster form. (b) Find \(A \cup B\), \(A \cap B\), \(A - B\) and \(B'\). (c) Verify \(n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)\). (d) Draw a Venn diagram showing every element of \(U\).

  13. (§2.4) (a) Decide whether each relation is a function, and give the domain and range of \(R_1\): \(R_1 = \{(1, 3), (2, 5), (3, 7), (4, 9)\}\); \(R_2 = \{(2, 1), (2, 3), (4, 5)\}\); \(R_3 = \{(x, y) \mid y = x^2,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}\); \(R_4 = \{(x, y) \mid x = y^2,\ x \ge 0\}\). (b) Is \(a * b = a + b - ab\) a binary operation on \(\mathbb{Z}\)? Compute \(3 * (-2)\). (c) Is division a binary operation on \(\mathbb{Z}\)? Explain.

  14. (§2.5) Write each statement in symbols, write its negation in symbols and in words, and decide which of the two is true: (a) “Every RTU student has a school ID.” (Let \(S\) = RTU students, \(I(x)\): \(x\) has a school ID.) (b) “There is a prime number that is even.” (c) “For every real number \(x\), \(x^2 + 1 > 0\).” (d) “Some integer \(n\) satisfies \(n^2 = 2\).”

  15. (§2.6, prove) Using the definitions “\(n\) is even if \(n = 2k\)” and “\(n\) is odd if \(n = 2k + 1\)” for some integer \(k\), prove that (a) the sum of two odd integers is even, and (b) the product of two odd integers is odd. Explain why checking ten examples would not be a proof.
